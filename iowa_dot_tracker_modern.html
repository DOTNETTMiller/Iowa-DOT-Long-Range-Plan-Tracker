Iowa DOT Artificial Intelligence Strategic Plan

FY 2026-2028

Executive Summary

Quick Overview of Iowa DOT AI Strategy

1. INTRODUCTION

The Iowa Department of Transportation (Iowa DOT) is responsible for planning, designing, building, operating, and maintaining a safe and reliable transportation system that supports Iowa's economy and quality of life. As transportation systems become increasingly complex and data-rich, Iowa DOT recognizes that traditional tools and processes alone are no longer sufficient to meet emerging challenges related to safety, mobility, system reliability, workforce capacity, and fiscal stewardship.

Artificial intelligence (AI) presents a significant opportunity to enhance how Iowa DOT delivers its mission. Properly applied, AI can help the department better anticipate safety risks, manage traffic and work zones in real time, optimize winter operations, improve construction oversight, and streamline internal business processes. At the same time, AI introduces new considerations related to data quality, transparency, privacy, cybersecurity, workforce impacts, and public trust.

This Artificial Intelligence Strategic Plan establishes a coordinated, agency-wide approach for how Iowa DOT will evaluate, deploy, govern, and scale AI capabilities over the next three fiscal years. It provides a common framework for decision-making, investment prioritization, and risk management while allowing flexibility for innovation across divisions and programs.

2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

2.1 Vision for Artificial Intelligence at Iowa DOT

Iowa DOT will leverage artificial intelligence to improve transportation safety, reliability, and service delivery by augmenting human expertise with data-driven insights, while ensuring responsible, transparent, and secure use of technology.

2.2 Why Artificial Intelligence Matters for Iowa DOT

  • Increasing System Complexity: Iowa's transportation system generates more data than ever before from traffic detectors, cameras, connected vehicles, weather systems, and maintenance equipment.
  • Safety Imperatives: AI can help identify high-risk conditions, predict emerging safety issues, and support proactive interventions.
  • Workforce Capacity: AI can automate time-consuming tasks, allowing staff to focus on higher-value activities.
  • Operational Resilience: AI-enabled tools improve response to incidents, severe weather, and unexpected disruptions.

2.3 Strategic Goals (2026-2028)

Goal 1: Improve Safety and System Reliability

AI will enhance detection and prediction of crashes, incidents, and near-misses. Improve work zone and worker safety. Support winter operations decisions that reduce weather-related crashes.

Goal 2: Strengthen Data and Digital Infrastructure

Improve data consistency, standards, and documentation. Increase accessibility of high-value datasets for analytics and AI while maintaining security and privacy.

Goal 3: Empower the Workforce

Provide AI literacy training. Establish clear guidance on acceptable use of AI tools. Support a culture of experimentation that encourages learning while managing risk.

Goal 4: Ensure Responsible and Trustworthy Use of AI

Implement governance processes. Maintain human oversight. Protect privacy and comply with all applicable laws and policies.

3. THREE-YEAR ROADMAP

Phase 1: Build the Foundation and Launch Flagship Pilots

  • Establish AI governance processes and system inventory
  • Advance data governance, cataloging, and standards
  • Launch high-impact AI pilots with clear success metrics
  • Provide initial AI training and guidance to staff

Phase 2: Scale Successful Solutions and Integrate into Operations

  • Expand successful pilots into production systems
  • Integrate AI tools into existing workflows and operational platforms
  • Broaden training and adoption across divisions
  • Refine governance based on lessons learned

Phase 3: Optimize, Mature, and Innovate

  • Optimize and refine deployed AI systems
  • Explore more advanced or complex use cases
  • Assess long-term organizational and technology needs for AI
  • Prepare the next iteration of the AI Strategic Plan

4. DEFINITION OF ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE

Working Definition: Artificial intelligence refers to computer-based systems that can perform or assist with tasks that normally require human intelligence, including recognizing patterns, understanding language, making predictions, recommending actions, or generating content, by learning from data or applying advanced computational models.

Categories of AI Relevant to Iowa DOT:

Machine Learning

Algorithms that learn patterns from historical data. Applications: Predicting crash likelihood, forecasting travel times, identifying anomalous patterns in construction data.

Computer Vision

AI models that interpret images and video. Applications: Detecting wrong-way vehicles, assessing pavement condition, monitoring work zones for safety compliance.

Natural Language Processing (NLP)

AI that understands and generates human language. Applications: Searching technical manuals, classifying customer inquiries, extracting data from narrative reports.

Optimization & Prescriptive Analytics

Models that recommend optimal actions. Applications: Optimizing winter maintenance routes, scheduling maintenance activities, recommending traffic management strategies.

Generative AI

Models that create new content (text, images, code). Applications: Drafting routine correspondence, summarizing documents, assisting with code development. Note: Requires human review and alignment with statewide policies.

5. AI GUIDING PRINCIPLES

Iowa DOT's approach to AI is guided by seven core principles that ensure responsible, effective, and trustworthy use:

1
Safety First

AI initiatives that directly support safety goals receive priority. All AI systems with safety implications undergo rigorous testing and human oversight.

2
Human-in-the-Loop

AI serves as a decision-support tool, not a replacement for professional judgment. Final decisions with safety, legal, or financial implications require human review.

3
Data Quality and Governance

Effective AI depends on high-quality, well-governed data. Data standards, documentation, security, and privacy protections are foundational.

4
Transparency and Explainability

AI systems should be understandable to users. Iowa DOT documents how AI systems work, what data they use, and how decisions are made.

5
Fairness and Equity

AI systems are evaluated for potential bias. Transportation services must be delivered equitably across all communities.

6
Privacy and Security

AI systems comply with all privacy laws and cybersecurity requirements. Personal data is protected and used only for authorized purposes.

7
Continuous Improvement

AI systems are monitored, evaluated, and refined over time. Performance metrics guide decisions about scaling, modification, or retirement.

6. AI GOVERNANCE FRAMEWORK

Iowa DOT has established a governance structure to ensure responsible AI adoption:

Risk-Based Approach

AI systems are categorized by risk level, with governance requirements scaled accordingly:

  • High Risk: Safety-critical applications, regulatory compliance, public-facing decisions → Extensive review, testing, oversight
  • Medium Risk: Operational support, internal processes → Standard review and monitoring
  • Low Risk: Productivity tools, document assistance → Lightweight approval with usage guidelines

Key Governance Elements

  • AI Use Case Inventory: Central registry of all AI systems in use or development
  • Review and Approval Process: Structured evaluation before deployment
  • Ongoing Monitoring: Performance tracking and bias detection
  • Documentation Requirements: Clear records of data sources, model logic, and decision processes
  • Training and Awareness: Staff understand AI capabilities, limitations, and ethical use
  • Alignment with State Policy: Compliance with Iowa enterprise AI guidance

7. SUCCESS METRICS

AI strategy success will be measured through:

Safety Outcomes
  • Crash reductions
  • Improved incident response times
  • Enhanced work zone safety
Operational Performance
  • System reliability improvements
  • Efficiency gains
  • Resource optimization
Workforce Impacts
  • Time savings from automation
  • Training completion rates
  • User adoption and satisfaction
Governance Effectiveness
  • % of AI systems reviewed
  • Compliance with standards
  • Risk management effectiveness

This is a living document. The AI Strategic Plan will be reviewed and updated regularly as technologies, policies, and use cases evolve. For the complete document including detailed use cases, governance frameworks, and implementation guidance, contact the Iowa DOT Planning Division.
